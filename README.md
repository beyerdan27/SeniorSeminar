# SeniorSeminar
<br>
The initial approach for this project, at least the session-scheduling aspect, is based on overlap. We calculate the overlap of any two of the 18 sesions in an ArrayList for lookup using the # of students in both and the average priority of that session in students' choices. We fill in the first two sessions in the top row based on the lowest overlap score. If any sessions require two instead of one due to anticipated popularity, that session will be the first one in the next row. Still in the top row, we calculate the sesion that overlaps the least with the two already in the top row, and repeat until the row is filled. The next row repeats this process, tarting with any alread-filled-in sessions in that row. Then, students are filled based on a priority score that prioritizes students with the greatest POTENTIAL overlap between their chosen sessions. Students are assigned to their first free choice in any row, and the first free session if there is no chosen session in a row. If a session is full and the current chose it, they take priority over the students who filled in randomly and did NOT choose it, bumping one of them to the next available session. Even if all the studentss in the session chose it, we still check if any of the students have a second choice in the row that also satisfies the requirements; if so, we bump them. The main downfall of this approach is the lack of consideration given to cross-row-dynamics: we don't adjust multiple rows at once or use multiple rows to inform our placements. This approach also uses ZERO randomization and ZERO brute force techniques. The main student placement method is recursive, but it does not brute force the schedule at all. This does mean it is likely a more optimal schedule exists that this program will NOT find.
